# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue with the code, not with
the person who wrote it.

This project takes patches from strangers and labels issues for people who have never
contributed to anything before, so the bar here is that a first-time contributor
should be able to get something wrong in public without it costing them anything.

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors and maintainers pledge to make participation in this
project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size,
visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and
expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality,
personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse,
inclusive and healthy community.

## What that looks like here

Examples of behaviour that helps:

- Reviewing a change on what it does, not on who sent it.
- Saying "this is wrong because X" rather than "this is wrong".
- Answering a question that has an obvious answer as if it did not.
- Accepting that a maintainer may decline a feature and still thank you for the work.

Examples of behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention of any kind.
- Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as a physical or email address,
  without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussion, including in pull request review.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, release comments — and when someone is representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report anything unacceptable by opening a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/joshlin2201/itspaint/security/advisories/new),
which is the one channel here that is not public and does not require sharing an email
address with anyone. Reports will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly,
and the reporter's privacy and security will be respected.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this in good faith may face temporary or
permanent consequences as determined by other maintainers.

### What happens next

1. **Correction.** A private, written warning, with clarity about why the behaviour
   was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.
2. **Warning.** A warning with consequences for continued behaviour, including no
   interaction with the people involved for a stated period.
3. **Temporary ban.** A temporary ban from any interaction with the project.
4. **Permanent ban.** A permanent ban from any public interaction with the project.
